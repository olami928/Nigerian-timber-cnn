Before you capture.

Better input reduces avoidable error.

  1. Expose a clean cross-sectionMake or select a flat end cut and clear debris.
  2. Stabilise and light the surfaceUse diffuse light; confirm pores and rays are sharp.
  3. Frame the wood, not the backgroundFill the frame and keep the camera square to the surface.
  4. Review, then analyseReplace blurred or poorly lit images before running analysis.

Species catalogue

Names, wood type and documented applications come from the local catalogue (literature and database records), not from image analysis.

Method and limitations

How a result is produced
The image is divided into a 6 × 8 grid (48 patches). Each 112 × 112 patch is classified with ShuffleNetV2, and the species is chosen by majority vote.
Patch agreement
The count of regions voting for the reported species. It measures internal consistency, not calibrated probability, and not correctness.
Research prototype
Results apply only to the 29-species Ibadan end-grain scope and should be read as a research aid, not a verdict.
Appropriate use
Not for legal, regulatory, purchasing, import/export or structural-grading decisions.
Read full limitations and evaluation

Reported independent-specimen image-level accuracy was 12.1% (ShuffleNetV2, primary) and 11.3% (ResNet18, comparison) on 611 held-out images from 14 species. Fifteen single-specimen species lacked independent-specimen test results. The model cannot reliably identify species outside the defined set.
