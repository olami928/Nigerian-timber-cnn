How a result is produced

  1. Acquire a transverse (end-grain) photograph.
  2. Divide the image into a 6 × 8 grid (48 patches, raster order).
  3. Classify each 112 × 112 normalized patch with ShuffleNetV2.
  4. Aggregate by majority vote; report agreement and top matches.

Field guide

Photographing the transverse end-grain

The model compares anatomical patterns visible on the cross-section. Correct surface selection and image quality are essential for a meaningful observation.

Recommended practice

  • Surface: transverse (end-grain / cross-section) only.
  • Preparation: flat, clean, freshly exposed cut; remove loose dust without sealing pores.
  • Framing: wood fills most of the frame; camera held square to the face.
  • Lighting & focus: diffuse, even light; vessels and rays clearly resolved.

Unsuitable images

  • Wrong plane: tangential/radial side grain, bark, or oblique views.
  • Occlusion: fingers, tools, labels, paint, soil, or moisture on the surface.
  • Artifacts: motion blur, defocus, specular glare, or heavy shadow.
  • Scale: distant or tightly cropped views where pore structure is unclear.

What “transverse end-grain” means

Imagine cutting across the trunk like slicing a loaf: the exposed face shows growth rings, vessels (pores) and rays as patterns on the cross-section. This is different from the long-grain patterns seen on planks (side grain). Timber ID is trained only on transverse end-grain surfaces.

  1. Expose a clean cross-section

    Make or select a flat end cut and clear debris so anatomical features remain visible.

  2. Stabilise and light the surface

    Hold steady under diffuse light; confirm the texture is sharp before capture.

  3. Frame the wood, not the background

    Fill the frame with timber and keep the camera approximately perpendicular.

  4. Review, then analyse

    Replace blurred or poorly lit images; better input reduces avoidable error.

Methodology & context

About this instrument

Nigerian Timber ID is an academic research prototype for macroscopic identification of Nigerian timber species from transverse end-grain images, with documented species information presented as reference.

Model and preprocessing

Each image is divided into a 6 × 8 grid (48 patches). Patches are resized to 112 × 112 pixels and normalized with ImageNet mean and standard deviation. A ShuffleNetV2 classifier (primary; ResNet18 used for comparison in evaluation) produces per-patch predictions aggregated by majority vote with mean-probability tie-breaking to yield an image-level suggestion and patch agreement.

Dataset and scope

Scope covers 29 hardwood species traded in Ibadan wood markets, photographed on transverse end-grain surfaces. The system does not recognize softwoods or species outside this set. Evaluation followed a specimen-aware protocol consistent with the project design.

Interpreting outputs

Patch agreement is the proportion/count of 48 regions voting for the reported species. It is a measure of internal consistency, not a calibrated probability and not a guarantee of correctness. Top matches and mean probabilities are supplementary. Documented applications and end-uses are literature/database records associated with the species and are not predicted from the photograph.

Privacy and appropriate use

Designed for local processing: images are handled on-device and are not transmitted to external servers by the application. Outputs must not be used for legal, regulatory, purchasing, import/export, or structural-grading decisions.

Model limitations and research notes

Read full limitations (important for interpretation)

Performance and status: Reported independent-specimen image-level accuracy was 12.1% (ShuffleNetV2, primary) and 11.3% (ResNet18, comparison) on 611 held-out images from 14 species. Fifteen single-specimen species lacked independent-specimen test results. Predictions can be incorrect and should be interpreted with caution.

Scope boundary: Results apply only to the defined 29-species Ibadan transverse end-grain scope. The model cannot reliably identify species outside this set.

Appropriate use: Not for legal, regulatory, purchasing, import/export, or structural-grading decisions. Patch agreement and mean probability are model outputs, not guarantees of correctness.

Documented uses: End-use information is drawn from available literature/database records for the species; it is descriptive reference data, not a property inferred from the submitted image.
