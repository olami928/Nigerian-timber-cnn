2. Analyse

Identification process

  1. Acquire image. Capture the transverse (end-grain) surface, not side grain or bark.
  2. Extract patches. The image is divided into a 6 × 8 grid (48 regions) in raster order.
  3. Classify patches. Each region is resized to 112 × 112, ImageNet-normalized, and evaluated by ShuffleNetV2.
  4. Aggregate result. The image-level prediction is a majority vote across patches; patch agreement and top matches are reported.

Photograph quality guide

Suitable

  • Transverse/cross-section face clearly visible
  • Flat, clean cut; surface fills the frame
  • Sharp focus with even, diffuse lighting
  • Camera approximately perpendicular to surface

Unsuitable

  • Side grain, bark, or angled/oblique view
  • Blur, glare, heavy shadow, dirt, or moisture
  • Wood area too small or pores not visible
  • Hands, tools, or marks obscuring the surface

Capture guidance

How to photograph transverse end-grain

The classifier relies on anatomical features visible on the cross-section. Image quality and correct surface selection are critical for meaningful output.

Recommended

  • Surface: transverse (end-grain/cross-section) only.
  • Preparation: flat, clean, freshly exposed cut; remove loose dust without blocking pores.
  • Framing: wood fills most of the frame; keep camera square to the surface.
  • Exposure: sharp focus, even lighting, minimal shadow or specular glare.

Avoid

  • Wrong view: tangential/radial side grain, bark, or heavily angled shots.
  • Occlusion: fingers, tools, labels, paint, soil, or moisture on the surface.
  • Artifacts: motion blur, defocus, strong reflections, or extreme darkness.
  • Scale: distant or tightly cropped images where pore structure is unclear.

  1. Expose a clean cross-section

    Use a flat end cut and clear debris so anatomical features are visible.

  2. Stabilize and light the surface

    Hold steady under diffuse light; confirm vessels and rays appear sharp before capture.

  3. Frame the wood surface

    Fill the frame with timber, avoiding background clutter and obstructions.

  4. Review and submit

    Replace blurry or poorly lit images; better input reduces avoidable errors.

Methodology & limitations

About Timber ID

Timber ID is an academic research prototype for machine-learning-based identification of Nigerian timber species from macroscopic transverse end-grain images, with documented species information presented for reference.

Model and preprocessing

Images are analysed as a 6 × 8 grid (48 patches). Each patch is resized to 112 × 112 pixels and normalized with ImageNet mean/std. The ShuffleNetV2 classifier produces per-patch predictions aggregated by majority vote (with mean-probability tie-breaking) to yield the image-level result and patch agreement.

Dataset scope

29 timber species traded in Ibadan wood markets, photographed on transverse end-grain surfaces. All species in scope are hardwoods; the system does not recognize softwoods or species outside this set. Splits and evaluation follow the project’s specimen-aware protocol.

Interpreting outputs

Patch agreement is the proportion/count of 48 sections voting for the reported species. It is not a calibrated probability and not a guarantee of correctness. Top matches and mean probabilities are supplementary indicators. Documented applications/end-uses are literature/database records for the species and are not predicted from the photograph.

Privacy and appropriate use

Designed for local processing: images are handled on-device by the application and are not transmitted to external servers. Do not use outputs for legal, regulatory, purchasing, import/export, or structural-grading purposes.

Model limitations and research notes

Research prototype — read limitations (important)

Performance and status: Reported independent-specimen image-level accuracy is 12.1% (ShuffleNetV2, primary) and 11.3% (ResNet18, comparison) on 611 held-out images from 14 species. Fifteen single-specimen species lacked independent-specimen test results. Predictions can be incorrect and should be interpreted with caution.

Scope: Results apply only to the defined 29-species Ibadan transverse end-grain scope. The model cannot reliably identify species outside this set.

Appropriate use: Not for legal, regulatory, purchasing, import/export, or structural-grading decisions. Patch agreement/mean probability are model outputs, not guarantees of correctness.
